# Competitive analysis: the Tavern lineage and character-writing practice

Scope note on method: every row below is labeled **[DOC]** documented fact (primary source: spec, official docs, source code/README), **[FOLK]** community folklore/consensus (guides, forum posts, no controlled test), **[STUDY]** peer-reviewed/preprint measurement, or **[INFER]** my inference from the above. Where a specific claim could not be sourced, it says "not found" rather than being filled in.

## 3. Cross-cutting: what works, what fails, what nobody does

**What works (best-sourced):**
- **Showing behavior via example dialogue outperforms declarative trait lists for getting the model to imitate a style** — this is the shared rationale behind Ali:Chat, and is corroborated independently by SillyTavern's own documentation: "the model is more likely to pick up the style and length constraints from the first message than anything else" [ST Character Design](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/core-concepts/characterdesign/). **[DOC + FOLK convergence]**.
- **Fixed-depth reinjection (Author's Note / Character's Note / KoboldAI's "A/N Strength") measurably combats dilution** by re-asserting trait text near the generation point regardless of chat length — documented mechanism in both ST and KoboldAI docs, and consistent with the *cause* identified in the academic literature below (attention decay over distance). **[DOC]**.
- **Separating permanent identity from situational context** (Description vs. Scenario) is repeated consistently across Chub, Backyard, and ST guidance as a maintainability practice, independent of surface syntax. **[FOLK, but convergent across independently-run apps]**.

**What fails (documented or measured):**
- **Persona/instruction drift over multi-turn dialogue is real and measured**, not just anecdote: Li et al., "Measuring and Controlling Persona Drift in Language Model Dialogs" (Harvard/Northeastern, arXiv:2402.10962) built a deterministic probe-question benchmark across 100 system prompts (including character-definition-style prompts) and found instruction-following degraded by round 8 of an 8-round dialogue, driven by **attention decay** — system-prompt tokens receive proportionally less attention as the conversation lengthens — [ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.10962](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.10962). **[STUDY]**.
- **Alignment-induced "positivity"/agreeableness bias in generated characters is documented**: Qraitem, Saenko & Plummer, "Breaking the Assistant Mold" (arXiv:2601.03396) name two specific failure patterns — a **positive moral bias** (characters uniformly take agreeable moral stances) and a **helpful-assistant bias** (characters always answer directly, never refuse/deflect), tracing the cause to maximum-likelihood pretraining plus assistant fine-tuning, and propose a "PersonaWeaver" framework separating world-building from behavioral generation as a partial fix — [arxiv.org/abs/2601.03396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03396). **[STUDY]** — this is close to a direct citation for GGBC's "positivity bias" failure mode.
- **The first message's style/length disproportionately anchors the model**, for better or worse — documented directly in ST's own docs, and echoed as folklore guidance ("if your rules say formal but your first message is casual, the model follows the example, not the rule"). **[DOC + FOLK]**.
- **Lorebook/World Info keyword activation has multiple documented false-trigger surfaces**: "match whole words" interacting badly with languages without whitespace word boundaries (e.g., Japanese/Chinese), sticky entries bypassing probability rolls on subsequent scans, vector-storage retrieval being fundamentally unpredictable ("impossible to predict exactly what entries will be inserted"), and outlet macros inside WI entries being explicitly unsupported because they can cause infinite recursion loops — all from [ST World Info docs](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/core-concepts/worldinfo/). **[DOC]**.
- **Community-reported drift is filed as a live defect, not just theorized**: Agnai has an open GitHub issue literally titled "Character personality loss during chat" — [agnai#301](https://github.com/agnaistic/agnai/issues/301). **[DOC — filed issue]**.
- **Format folklore around W++ decline could not be verified at primary-source quality** — every "W++ is dead / brackets confuse the model" claim I found traces back to low-quality SEO blogs (mini-tavern.com, tavernsprite.com) rather than a primary community post, spec discussion, or measurement. The strongest evidence of W++'s decline is *structural*: the two most-cited primary teaching guides (Trappu's, kingbri's) simply don't use or mention it. **[INFER]** decline is real (visible in guide content), but the *stated reason* for decline is unverified folklore.

**What nobody does (the gap this initiative would fill):**
- **No app or spec in this lineage runs scenario-probe testing, behavioral scoring, or consistency validation on a character card before/after publishing it.** Every "testing" tool found (e.g., `RisuAI_bot_test`) tests *scripting logic* (regex/CBS/lorebook wiring), not *character behavior* — confirmed by reading its own README, which describes it as a dev tool for "writ[ing] and test[ing] bot settings, regex, lorebook, and CBS scripts," with no mention of output evaluation — [tammy3211/RisuAI_bot_test](https://github.com/tammy3211/RisuAI_bot_test). **[DOC — confirms absence]**.
- **No card format encodes a decision framework, conflict-resolution pattern, or explicit value hierarchy as first-class structured fields.** Every field added from V1→V2→V3 is either prose (description/personality/scenario), lorebook/example-dialogue content, or metadata/provenance/assets (V3's entire addition list). **[DOC — derived from the spec diff in §1]**.
- **Per-field token counts exist widely (ST core + extensions like SillyTavern-CharacterTools/AlternateDescriptions) as an authoring aid, but none of it is tied to any behavioral or consistency metric** — it is pure token accounting (a warning when a character eats more than ~50% of context), not a quality signal. Confirms the "prove or disprove" ask: **token attribution exists; behavioral/consistency validation does not**, anywhere surveyed. **[DOC + INFER]**.
- **Academic role-play benchmarks exist (CharacterEval, RMTBench, RVBench, WikiRoleEval) but live entirely outside the Tavern lineage** — none integrate with SillyTavern/Risu/Agnai/Chub/Backyard as a pre-publish or in-app check; they are research-only eval harnesses, per their own materials (e.g., [CharacterEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.01275v1), [RMTBench](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/rmtbench)). **[DOC — confirms the ecosystem/research split]**.

## 4. Implications for a behavioral-definition methodology (≤10 bullets, each tied to a cited fact)

1. **Build in a fixed-depth reinjection mechanism by default**, not an optional add-on — ST's Character's Note and KoboldAI's A/N Strength exist precisely because static system-prompt text loses influence over distance, which Li et al. measured directly as attention decay by round 8 of dialogue ([arXiv:2402.10962](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.10962); [ST Author's Note docs](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/core-concepts/authors-note/)).
2. **Treat the first assistant-authored example as load-bearing, not decorative** — ST's own docs state the model imitates first-message style/length "more than anything else," so a behavioral spec must generate or validate example turns as part of the compiled artifact, not leave them to the character sheet's prose fields ([ST Character Design](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/core-concepts/characterdesign/)).
3. **Explicitly counter-engineer the positivity/agreeableness bias** — this is not folklore but a named, mechanism-explained failure (positive moral bias + helpful-assistant bias, rooted in RLHF/instruction-tuning) with a proposed fix pattern (separating world-building from behavioral/moral generation) directly transferable to a "core values / conflict pattern" field design ([arXiv:2601.03396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03396)).
4. **A card format alone cannot fix drift — reinforcement architecture matters more than syntax.** No format survey found (W++, PList, Ali:Chat) makes a drift-resistance claim backed by measurement; the only measured drift-mitigation lever in the whole lineage is *injection depth/strength*, a runtime mechanism, not a card field ([ST/KoboldAI docs, §1]).
5. **Ship per-field token accounting, but do not mistake it for a quality gate** — every app surveyed with token counters (ST + extensions) uses them only to warn against context bloat, never to signal behavioral quality; a v2 architecture should add a genuinely new signal (consistency/behavioral score) rather than duplicate existing token UI ([SillyTavern-CharacterTools README, §1/§3]).
6. **Nothing in this lineage validates a card before publishing it** — the closest thing (`RisuAI_bot_test`) tests scripting wiring, not behavior, confirmed from its own README; this is a genuinely open competitive space, not one where GGBC would be reinventing an existing tool ([tammy3211/RisuAI_bot_test](https://github.com/tammy3211/RisuAI_bot_test)).
7. **Borrow the V2/V3 spec's separation of "prompt content" vs. "discoverable-but-excluded metadata"** (`creator_notes` is explicitly barred from the prompt) as a model for keeping behavioral scaffolding (e.g., internal decision-framework notes) out of the token-costed path while still being inspectable ([spec_v2.md](https://github.com/malfoyslastname/character-card-spec-v2/blob/main/spec_v2.md)).
8. **Adopt V3's provenance pattern (`source`, timestamps, non-user-editable, append-only) for behavioral-definition versioning** — if a compiled behavioral profile is regenerated, an append-only lineage field prevents silent divergence between "what was tested" and "what's live," mirroring V3's attribution-chain intent ([SPEC_V3.md](https://github.com/kwaroran/character-card-spec-v3/blob/main/SPEC_V3.md)).
9. **Expect lorebook/keyword mechanisms to misfire in exactly the ways already documented** (word-boundary failures in non-whitespace languages, unpredictable vector retrieval, sticky-entry probability bypass, recursive-macro loops) if the behavioral architecture reuses World-Info-style triggering for value/conflict recall — these are documented ST failure modes, not hypothetical ones ([ST World Info docs](https://docs.sillytavern.app/usage/core-concepts/worldinfo/)).
10. **Community folklore is not a reliable basis for format decisions without re-verification** — the flagship claim in this space ("W++ is dead because brackets confuse the model") could not be traced to any primary source despite deliberate searching; GGBC's methodology should default to citable, ideally model-tested claims (per §2/§3) rather than repeating unverified format lore, consistent with this team's existing "prose rots / verify against source" practice.
